### Researcher Notes
- The raw series shows mixed evidence on stationarity, so differencing and scale-aware models should remain in the ablation set.
- First differences are stationary by both tests, which supports ARIMA-style specifications with at most one order of differencing.
- Strong lag-1 autocorrelation indicates persistence; naive, moving-average, ETS, and autoregressive baselines should be competitive.
- Trend strength is material, so trend-capable models such as ETS, ARIMA with drift, boosted trees on lagged features, and recurrent networks are justified.
- The 21-trading-day seasonal component is weak, so monthly seasonality should be treated as optional rather than assumed.
- The distribution contains tail events beyond 3 standard deviations, which argues for robust error tracking and possibly scaled targets for deep-learning models.
- A small number of weekend timestamps appear in the source data. Downstream models should treat the series as ordered trading observations rather than assume a perfectly regular business-day index.

## Preprocessing

### Decisions
- Missing values are filled with forward-fill followed by backfill to preserve temporal continuity while preventing leading gaps from leaking into downstream feature engineering.
- Outliers in `Market_Liquidity_Index` are capped with 1.5 x IQR fences rather than dropped so extreme liquidity stress periods remain represented without dominating scale-sensitive models.
- Lag features [1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 14, 21, 30] and rolling mean/std windows [7, 14, 30] were created to expose persistence, local trend, and volatility structure identified during EDA.
- Calendar features (`day_of_week`, `month`, `quarter`) were retained as low-cost seasonal signals even though the 21-day seasonal component looked weak in the initial diagnostics.
- The train/test split is strictly chronological at 80/20 with no shuffle to preserve time-series integrity and avoid look-ahead bias.
- Separate scaler bundles were saved for model families: `StandardScaler` for classical ML models and `MinMaxScaler` for deep-learning models. All scalers were fit on the training split only.

## Researcher Analysis Notes
*Compiled: 2026-03-28 — Full Ablation Study Post-Run*

### 1. The Persistence Trap
The raw series has lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.97. This creates a benchmark
problem: the naive last-value model achieves MAE=0.104, R2=0.634. Any model
that simply memorises recent values will appear competitive. This is why
ARIMA(1,1,1) — essentially a first-order random-walk correction — achieves
MAE=0.095, matching or beating far more complex architectures.

### 2. Linear Models Dominate the ML Family
Counter-intuitively, linear models (SVR-Linear, ElasticNet, Ridge) outperform
all tree-based models (XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting)
by a wide margin:
  - SVR (Linear): MAE=0.0935
  - XGBoost: MAE=0.1647 (76% worse)
  - LightGBM: MAE=0.1472 (57% worse)

Root cause: The liquidity index exhibits a strong, smooth trend (R2=0.81 over
time). Tree-based models cannot extrapolate beyond the training range — they
plateau at the last seen value level. Linear models extrapolate the learned
trend into the test period. This is a canonical failure mode of tree-based
models on trending time series.

Implication for practitioners: Always check for structural trend before
defaulting to gradient boosted trees in financial time series.

### 3. Deep Learning Depth Penalizes Performance
Among RNN architectures, vanilla single-layer models beat deeper variants:
  - GRU (vanilla): MAE=0.110, R2=0.721
  - Stacked LSTM (2L): MAE=0.351, R2=-0.76 (WORSE than mean baseline!)
  - Deep LSTM (3L+BN): MAE=0.482, R2=-2.10

Deeper models overfit with only 2668 training samples. The negative R2 values
indicate predictions worse than simply predicting the test-set mean. Dropout
at 40% collapsed the LSTM entirely (MAE=0.650, R2=-3.89).

The best RNN (GRU vanilla, MAE=0.110) still loses to the best statistical
model (ARIMA, MAE=0.095). This is the sample efficiency problem.

### 4. Transformer Surprises
The 4-head Transformer with sinusoidal positional encoding tied the best RNN
model at MAE=0.114 despite running the full 150 epochs (no early stopping
triggered). This suggests:
  (a) The model is still improving at epoch 150 — more training may help
  (b) Multi-head self-attention finds useful cross-lag relationships
  (c) Positional encoding captures the temporal ordering efficiently

The CNN+Transformer hybrid was the worst advanced DL model (R2=-0.004),
confirming that stacking CNN with Transformer without careful architectural
choices hurts more than helps.

### 5. WaveNet Outperforms Attention Models
WaveNet-style dilated causal CNNs (MAE=0.137) outperformed all attention-based
variants (Attention LSTM MAE=0.199, Attention BiLSTM MAE=0.184). This aligns
with WaveNet literature — the gated activation units and skip connections
provide an efficient inductive bias for autoregressive time series without
requiring the parameter count of attention mechanisms.

### 6. Ensemble Behavior
- Simple Average (35 models): MAE=0.142 — WORSE than the best single model
  (pollution from bad models like Deep LSTM and dropout LSTM)
- Top-5 Average: MAE=0.094 — competitive with the best individual model
- Top-3 Average: MAE=0.094 — marginal benefit over Top-5
- Stacking (non-leaky): MAE=0.164 for Ridge — WORSE than simple averaging
  The non-leaky stacking uses simple average for the first 50% of test and
  meta-learner for the second 50%. The poor performance suggests the
  meta-learner does not generalize well with only 333 training points.

Key insight: Ensemble gains require model diversity AND model quality. Including
poorly performing models (negative R2) into a simple average ensemble actively
harms performance. Selective ensemble (Top-K) is a more robust strategy.

### 7. Directional Accuracy Analysis
Most models achieve 55-62% DA on the test set (chance=50%). Notable:
  - GRU (vanilla): 62.4% — highest among RNNs
  - Linear Regression: 60.1% — best among linear models
  - Naive model: 0% — by construction (predicts no change = 0 direction)
  - Transformer: 60.9%

Directional accuracy of ~60% over a 667-day test period suggests genuine
predictive signal beyond the random walk hypothesis for the liquidity index.

The Support Vector Regressor with a linear kernel is the overall winner.

  - MAE=0.0935 (best across all 42 models)
  - RMSE=0.1576, R2=0.7470, DA=59.2%
  
The linear kernel is optimal here because the lag+rolling feature space is
already highly predictive and linear — no non-linear transformation is needed.

4. ARIMAX / SARIMAX with exogenous regressors
5. Walk-forward expanding window evaluation for robust out-of-sample testing
